## Work Completed

### The pricing check changed the answer

WhatsApp is the obvious channel for a South African small business, so it was worth checking rather than assuming. Current Meta pricing makes it the wrong choice **for alerts specifically**:

- WhatsApp's free window opens only when a *customer* messages the business, and lasts 24 hours from their last message.
- An escalation alert goes *to the owner* and is business-initiated. No window is open, so it bills as a utility template on every send, with no free tier.
- It also requires Meta business verification and pre-approved templates.
- From 1 October 2026, even utility templates inside the service window lose their free status.

So WhatsApp would have meant real per-alert cost plus heavy onboarding, to tell an owner someone is waiting.

**Telegram** does the same job for nothing: free, unlimited, no verification, no template approval, and a genuine push notification. A bot token takes minutes. The honest trade-off is that the recipient must use Telegram, which is why **email stays the required baseline** and Telegram is the recommended addition (DEC-0025).

Worth recording for later: WhatsApp is still the right integration in the *opposite* direction. Inbound customer messages open a free window, so replies inside it cost nothing. As a **customer** channel the economics are reversed — that is on the roadmap.

### The structural fix that mattered more

Last session I made production refuse to boot without Twilio, because the dashboard hardcoded an SMS option and DEC-0017 says the interface must not promise what the system cannot do.

That had the dependency backwards. It meant adding any channel to the UI made every deployment without that provider fail to start — the interface dictating infrastructure. The fix is to let the UI reflect the configuration: available channels are now derived from the senders actually constructed, passed through to the dashboard, and `addRecipient` rejects anything else. DEC-0017 is upheld more directly, since an owner is never shown a channel whose alerts would fail, and no provider is mandatory.

### My own validator caught me

Writing DEC-0025 I put "Partially supersedes DEC-0022 (…)" in the Supersedes field. `validate_decisions.py` rejected it: supersession must be a parseable Decision ID, not prose. Fixed to a plain `DEC-0022` with the scope narrowed in a note beneath. Small, but the governance tooling doing its job unprompted is the point of having it.
